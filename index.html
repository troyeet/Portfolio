The Grand Arena

The building at the top of this page. Have a look around it first — the reading can wait.

A full arena, built in Blender: the bowl, the seating, the stairs, and the roof over the top of it. You can circle it from outside, then drop down onto the pitch and look straight back up.

Most 3D you see on a website is really a video of a model, or a photo of one. This is the actual model, and it is running on your machine right now — which is why you can point the camera wherever you like instead of watching where someone else pointed it.

From Blender to your browser

How the arena got from my computer onto your screen. Five steps, no magic.

  1. Model

    I build the arena in Blender — bowl, seats, stairs, roof. Modelled so the camera can go inside it, not just circle the outside.

    Blender scene

  2. Export

    Saved out as a .glb — the standard file for 3D on the web. One file holding the shapes, the colours and the lights together.

    grand-arena.glb, 3.1 MB

  3. Parse

    Your browser opens that file and reads it back into a building: every piece, where it sits, and what it is made of.

    1,504 pieces

  4. Merge

    Those pieces get stitched into one before anything is drawn. It is the step that keeps the page quick instead of crawling.

    One piece, not 1,504

  5. Draw

    Painted onto the page, live. The moment you stop moving it, it stops working — so an open tab is not quietly eating your battery.

    What you are dragging
